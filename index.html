Why Global is priced above India

Global sits at about 2.2× the India price at every tier, on purpose. Agency is ₹4,499 in India against $119 globally — about ₹9,900 at 83 to the dollar. India is price-led and volume-led. The US and EU already pay $199–$399 a seat for Sprout and Later. Same product, different willingness to pay.

Enforce this at the billing entity, not the browser. INR needs an Indian billing address with a GSTIN or an Indian card. Everyone else is on the global card. Gate on IP and the India price leaks worldwide through VPNs within a month.

Add-ons

Everything bills in units so the prorated seat flow you already have can handle rooms too. One member is one unit. One social room is four. Bundled tiers price units below list, so upgrading stays cheaper than stacking add-ons.

How this stays friendly

The risk with charging per room and per member is that it reads as nickel-and-diming. Four choices defuse that. Workspaces are unlimited and free on every paid plan, so internal work never costs more. Client and guest viewers are free and unlimited — the loudest agency complaint about competitors. Flo is described as included, never as a meter. And going over a limit never deletes anything.

Downgrades go read-only, never destructive. Rooms and workspaces past the limit lock with a restore prompt. Data stays. A customer who churns and returns finds their board as they left it.

What to watch after launch

Free-to-paid conversion should sit above 4%, and the second social room is the trigger to watch most closely. Share of paid brands exceeding their Flo pool: below 5% means the allowance feels free; above 15% means you are gating a feature you marketed as included. Net revenue retention above 110% tells you rooms and seats are growing inside accounts. Watch Flo cost as a share of revenue against 6–10% in India and 3–5% globally. Treat drift upward as a pricing bug, not an infra one.
